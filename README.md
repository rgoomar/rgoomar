# Hey, I'm Rishi

I'm a Staff Software Engineer in Chicago. I started on the front end, moved into full-stack with JavaScript and Java, and kept getting pulled further down the stack into CI/CD, databases, cloud infrastructure, and site reliability. These days I also spend a lot of time on developer experience and AI tooling, focused on making complex systems calmer to run, easier to ship on, and cheaper to operate.

I don't see systems and people as separate problems. Reliable software depends on clear plans, useful tools, focused time, and teams that trust each other enough to ask hard questions.

## How I work

**Follow curiosity all the way down.** At my first job, I earned the nickname "Spelunker" because I was always digging through unfamiliar parts of the codebase. That instinct never went away. I like understanding how things actually work across layers, tracking down the real root cause, and sharing what I learn with the people around me.

**Fix the pattern, not just the fire.** Putting out an incident matters, but leaving behind better tooling, documentation, or shared defaults is what keeps the team from fighting the same problem twice.

**Build environments where people can do their best work.** I care a lot about giving teammates clear expectations, honest context, and the room to challenge assumptions and take ownership.

## What I build on the side

Most of my side projects start as something I wish existed for myself:

- [MacCaffeine](https://github.com/rgoomar/maccaffeine) is a tiny Swift menu-bar app that keeps your Mac awake using the same power assertions as `caffeinate`, without needing admin access or touching system settings. It adds timers, staying awake while a specific app runs, and automatic cutoffs if your battery gets low or the Mac runs hot.
- [Hype Doc](https://myhypedoc.com) is a calm place to track personal and professional wins. I wanted somewhere I could query and add to from work, AI tools, the web, or mobile, and nothing out there felt right, so I built it with Ruby on Rails, Hotwire Native, and PostgreSQL.
- [aws-cct](https://github.com/rgoomar/aws-cct) started as a Python script I wrote to compare AWS costs month over month and spot which services were spiking. I ported it to Go and packaged it as a CLI with Homebrew and GoReleaser so anyone can use it.
